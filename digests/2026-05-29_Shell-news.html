ARC Resources: Four-Regulator Approval Gauntlet Ahead of July Shareholder Vote

Shell's $22B (including debt) acquisition of ARC Resources faces a multi-track regulatory process that goes well beyond the July shareholder vote. Approvals required: (1) Canadian Competition Act; (2) Investment Canada Act — a national security review of foreign control over Canadian energy assets, a non-trivial hurdle in the current geopolitical climate; (3) Canada Transportation Act; (4) US Hart-Scott-Rodino. ARC shareholders need 66⅔% approval at the July special meeting, where they receive 0.40247 Shell shares + C$8.20 cash per ARC share (27% premium to April 24 price). Deal adds ~370,000 boe/d and deepens Shell's Montney position. Closing targeted H2 2026. Advisory angle: The ICA national security track is the least predictable variable — Canadian government scrutiny of foreign energy ownership has tightened since 2022. Track closely.

Dutch Supreme Court Climate Ruling Watch: Advisory Opinion Due H2 2026, Final Decision Early 2027

The Supreme Court of the Netherlands held oral arguments on May 22, marking the first time any nation's Supreme Court has considered whether a company has a civil law obligation to reduce CO₂ emissions. Milieudefensie demands 45% net emission reduction by 2030 (vs 2019), reinstating the 2021 district court order that the 2024 Court of Appeal had overturned. Shell's position: targeted orders against a single company are the "wrong tool" for a global transition. An Advocate General advisory opinion is expected H2 2026; a binding ruling follows in early 2027. Advisory angle: Regardless of outcome, this ruling shapes global precedent for corporate climate liability. If Shell loses, it creates an actionable legal template replicable against other majors across European jurisdictions. Wael Sawan's strategy — balancing energy delivery with transition targets — will face its starkest legal test yet.

LNG Canada Kitimat: Technical Challenges Emerge as Ramp-Up Hits Turbulence

Shell's flagship LNG Canada facility at Kitimat, BC — the most significant new LNG plant Shell has commissioned in years — is experiencing technical difficulties that led to at least one LNG tanker being diverted without a full cargo. Q1 2026 liquefaction volumes reached 7.9 MT but Q2 guidance holds a wide range of 7.6–8.0 MT, signalling management uncertainty about ramp-up pace. The plant's strategic importance was demonstrated when it partially compensated for Qatari supply disruptions during Q1 Middle East tensions. Advisory angle: LNG Canada is the linchpin of Shell's ARC acquisition rationale — ARC's Montney gas needs LNG Canada as the export outlet. Any sustained underperformance here undermines the integrated value thesis that Jefferies and other bulls are banking on. Monitor closely ahead of Q2 results (expected July 24).
