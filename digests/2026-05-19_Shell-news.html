Shell AGM: Follow This Climate Resolution Defeated — ~20% Investor Support Signals Persistent Pressure

Resolution 23 — co-filed by 27 institutional investors representing €1.5 trillion AUM, requesting Shell publish a report on shareholder value under IEA declining demand scenarios — was defeated today at Shell's hybrid AGM in London. For Accenture advisory purposes: the "20% persistent protest vote" pattern signals that Shell's investor base remains split between pragmatists backing Sawan's LNG-led pivot and a durable ESG coalition that will revisit this annually. Apollo, Blackstone and KKR Compete for Shell's $15B LNG Canada Stake — Strategic Portfolio Rebalancing

Shell is running a formal auction for up to ~30% of its 40% stake in LNG Canada (Phase 1 and Phase 2 bundled), with Apollo Global, Blackstone and KKR as the three remaining bidders. The deal is expected to value the asset at well north of $10B and potentially up to $15B. Shell's logic is clear: sell infrastructure yield (LNG terminal) to institutional capital hungry for long-duration cash flows, redeploy proceeds into upstream resource (ARC Resources), and retain operational control. This is the classic Sawan "capital efficiency" playbook — divest regulated/export assets, hold the molecules. For Accenture advisory: this transaction will require extensive digital and operational integration work; the new infrastructure owner will need operating model clarity on technology separation, data ownership, and control systems — a direct engagement opportunity.

ARC Acquisition Crystallised as 'Missing Piece' for LNG Canada Phase 2 Feed Gas — Strategy Rationale Sharpens

Industry analysts have crystallised the strategic logic behind Shell's $16.4B / $22B (including debt) ARC Resources acquisition announced April 27: ARC's Montney gas assets in northeast British Columbia provide the incremental feed gas volumes required to support a Final Investment Decision (FID) on LNG Canada Phase 2, potentially by year-end 2026. LNG Canada Phase 1 (which started production in 2024) reaches Asia-Pacific markets Shell cannot access via its existing European/Atlantic LNG portfolio. Phase 2 would double export capacity. The deal requires ARC shareholder approval at a special meeting expected July 2026, plus Canadian regulatory sign-offs (Competition Act, Investment Canada Act). Advisory note: the Montney-to-LNG-Canada-to-Asia chain is the spine of Shell's next decade of integrated gas growth — expect Sawan to champion this narrative heavily at investor days and in advisory engagements.

Shell-Keppel Immersion Cooling Testbed Goes Live — GTL Fluid Targets Data Centre AI Workloads
