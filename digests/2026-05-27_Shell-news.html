Shell and INEOS Forge Gulf of America Upstream Alliance — INEOS Takes 21% Stake in Appomattox-Area Plays

Shell and INEOS Energy have agreed to jointly pursue new deepwater oil and gas opportunities in the Gulf of America, centred on assets near Shell's existing Appomattox platform. INEOS acquires a 21% working interest in Shell's pre-FID Fort Sumter discovery plus the Sisco exploration well and a further exploration target to be drilled by 2030. The deal reinforces Shell's stated "high-grading" capital strategy — partnering to share exploration risk while maintaining operatorship of high-margin deepwater barrels. For Accenture advisors, this signals continued upstream capital concentration and de-emphasises lower-margin assets, consistent with the ARC Resources acquisition thesis.

Shell Completes ExCo Delayering Into Three Business Lines — P&T Integration Finalising in H1 2026

Wael Sawan is finalising the ExCo restructure that collapses Shell's leadership into three P&L units: Integrated Gas (Cedric Cremers), Upstream (Peter Costello), and Downstream, Renewables & Energy Solutions. Critically, the Projects & Technology directorate — historically a large shared-services function — is being absorbed into the three business lines in H1 2026, eliminating a layer of organisational friction. This is the most consequential operating model shift since Shell's 2022 unification. Advisory implication: procurement, IT, and digital transformation decisions will now be made closer to the business lines, changing the buyer landscape for Accenture's Shell engagement.

ARC Resources Acquisition on Track for H2 2026 Close — Lifts Shell Production CAGR to 4%

Shell's C$22 billion all-cash-and-shares deal for Canada's ARC Resources remains on schedule for H2 2026 completion. The transaction merges ARC's 1.5 million net Montney acres with Shell's 440,000 net acres, adding ~2 billion boe proved-plus-probable reserves and lifting production CAGR from 1% (2025 CMD guidance) to 4% through 2030. Shell has allocated ~$4 billion of its $24–26 billion 2026 capex envelope to the deal. No regulatory impediments have been flagged. Shell Relaunches Sprng Energy Sale — Plans Full India Renewables Exit; NIIF-Temasek Bid Leads at $1.6B+

Shell has appointed Barclays to manage a full divestment of Sprng Energy, its Indian renewables platform (2,300 MWp operational, 5,026 MWp contracted) originally acquired for $1.55 billion in 2022. A joint bid from India's National Investment and Infrastructure Fund (NIIF) and Singapore's Temasek is leading at over $1.6 billion — a thin premium on the original cost. The exit is part of Sawan's deliberate retreat from emerging-market renewables in favour of LNG trading and upstream. Advisory implication: Shell's low-carbon portfolio in Asia is contracting sharply; Accenture's decarbonisation advisory angles at Shell should now focus on operational efficiency and CCS rather than greenfield renewable build-out.

Shell's Energy Transition Strategy Wins 78% Shareholder Backing — Investor Coalition Backs Sawan's LNG-Led Roadmap

At the May 19 AGM, 78% of shareholders voted to endorse Shell's current energy transition strategy — characterised by reduced near-term carbon targets and a pivot toward LNG and upstream cash generation. Meanwhile the Follow This Paris-alignment resolution attracted only 13% support (down from 20.6% in 2025 and a 2021 peak of 30.5%), despite being co-filed by 23 institutional investors managing €1.5 trillion AUM, including current and former Shell employees for the first time. The sharp decline in activist support gives Sawan's management significant latitude through at least the 2027 AGM cycle.
