ARC Resources Acquisition: $1.5B Annual FCF, July Shareholder Vote on Track

Apollo, Blackstone & KKR Battle for Shell's 40% LNG Canada Stake — $10–15B Deal in Play

Three of the world's largest alternative asset managers are the remaining bidders in Shell's auction of a significant portion of its 40% stake in LNG Canada, with deal value expected between $10B and $15B. Wael Sawan stated Shell is "very comfortable" with its current stake and is "not necessarily looking to reduce" it — framing the process as recycling capital from mature assets to fund ARC and growth capex, not a strategic retreat. LNG Canada (Kitimat, BC) produces 14 MTPA from two trains (Train 2 ramped up Nov 2025) and sits on the Pacific gateway to Asia. A Phase 2 FID — which would double capacity to 28 MTPA — is expected by year-end 2026 and hinges partly on how Shell resolves its ownership position. For Accenture, this capital recycling move is the clearest signal of Shell's portfolio transformation thesis in action: concentrate on operatorship and project development, monetise infrastructure value to infrastructure capital.

Pearl GTL Train 2 Damage After Iran Attack on Ras Laffan — Up to 1-Year Repair Timeline

Iranian missile strikes on Ras Laffan Industrial City on 18 March 2026 damaged Shell's Pearl GTL facility — the world's largest gas-to-liquids plant (1.6 Bcf/d throughput, ~140 kbbl/d liquids output). Train 2 sustained direct damage with an estimated ~1 year to full repair; the entire facility halted production pending damage assessment. The strikes hit approximately 17% of Qatar's total LNG export capacity and represent the most significant energy infrastructure disruption in the region in decades. Pearl GTL is a high-margin business (premium base oils, synthetic fuels) and its absence will weigh on Downstream earnings into 2027. This event is reshaping how Shell and peer IOCs assess geopolitical risk premiums in Middle East asset valuations — relevant context when advising on Shell's portfolio risk framework.
