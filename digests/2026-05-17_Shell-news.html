AGM T-2: Sawan Pay 60% Rise Triggers Second Shareholder Battle Alongside Resolution 23

Resolution 23 Final Pre-Vote Push: 23 Institutions + Shell Employees Co-File; BP Comparison Sets Floor

Resolution 23 asks Shell to publish a report on how it would create shareholder value under IEA Stated Policies (STEPS) and Announced Pledges (APS) scenarios — i.e., scenarios where oil & gas demand structurally declines. Co-filers include 23 institutional investors (€1.5T AUM) and, for the first time, current and former Shell employees. The board unanimously recommends against. The BP comparator: At BP's April AGM, an identical Follow This resolution was blocked from the agenda; shareholders retaliated with an 18% vote against the chair's re-election and majority votes against two board resolutions. That precedent strengthens the argument for Shell to allow a clean vote rather than escalate governance risk. Minerva Analytics notes the language of Resolution 23 is deliberately moderate — it asks for a report, not a strategy change — which typically improves vote share. Expected outcome: ~20–30% support (vs. ~20% in 2025); a result above 30% would be a material governance signal. Accenture lens: Post-AGM, whichever direction the vote goes, Shell's C-suite will need a narrative on demand resilience — this is exactly the framing where Accenture's scenario planning capabilities are most relevant to the engagement.

Shell's 2026 Energy Security Scenarios: Archipelagos, Surge & Horizon — Data Centres Are a Swing Factor

Shell published its third Energy Security Scenarios edition in March 2026, mapping three futures to 2100. Surfacing now because Resolution 23 and the ARC acquisition acquisition narrative both live within this framework. The three paths: Archipelagos — geopolitical fragmentation, fossil-fuel entrenchment, +2.5°C by 2100; Surge — AI productivity boom drives hyperscaler-led renewables buildout, net zero by 2080, +2.1°C peak then falling; Horizon — normative 2050 net zero path requiring 140× scale-up of carbon capture (7 Gt/yr by 2050 vs. 50 Mt today). The Surge scenario is notable for Accenture: data centres consume ~5,000 TWh by 2050 (≈8% of global electricity), with tech companies becoming de facto energy developers. Shell's immersion cooling fluid (DLC Fluid S3) and LNG supply to power plants are positioned inside this scenario. Accenture lens: This is Shell's intellectual framing device for all C-suite strategy conversations — understanding which scenario Sawan believes Shell is navigating is essential for positioning Accenture's AI and energy transition advisory work. The Surge scenario is most consistent with Shell's ARC + LNG + data-centre-cooling portfolio.

Shell India Deploys AI Across Asset Portfolio: Production Optimisation, Safety Analytics & Predictive Maintenance

S&P Global's interview with Shell India leadership details one of the more concrete live AI deployments within the Shell enterprise. Use cases in production: real-time production rate optimisation via ML models trained on well and reservoir data; FPSO safety analytics (anomaly detection on sensor streams to pre-empt equipment failures); and predictive maintenance integrated with Yokogawa's machine-vision robotics platform. Shell India is also a pilot site for agentic AI workflows — combining the SLB Lumi data platform with internal Shell data lakes (trillions of data rows) to give subsurface engineers AI-assisted decision support. Shell's broader enterprise AI portfolio spans 100+ live or in-development applications. Accenture lens: Shell India is a referenceable deployment at scale. If Accenture is engaged on AI-for-operations scoping within Shell, the India use cases are the internal benchmark to beat on cycle time and cost per barrel — and represent a natural expansion node for Accenture's energy AI practice. The SLB/NVIDIA agentic layer remains the key integration point to track for architecture decisions in H2 2026.
