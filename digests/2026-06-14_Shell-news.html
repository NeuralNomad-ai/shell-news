ARC Resources Files Shareholder Circular for Shell Acquisition — $16.4B Deal Goes to Vote July 14; 66.7% Threshold Required

ARC Resources Ltd. filed its management information circular on June 12, formally setting a virtual special shareholder meeting for July 14, 2026 (10:00 a.m. Mountain Time). The circular lays out the full plan of arrangement with Shell plc and Shell Canada Limited. Deal terms: ARC shareholders receive 0.40247 Shell ordinary shares plus C$8.20 in cash per ARC share (total consideration ~C$32.80/share at announcement, implying ~$16.4B enterprise value). Approval threshold: two-thirds (66⅔%) of votes cast. Proxy voting deadline: July 10 at 10:00 a.m. MT. An amending agreement was signed June 6 to address mechanics around the delivery of Shell consideration shares. ARC's board unanimously recommends approval. Transaction close expected H2 2026, subject to court, Competition Bureau Canada, and Investment Canada Act clearances. The deal would add approximately 370,000 boe/d of production capacity — primarily Montney natural gas — cementing Shell's position as a major LNG feedstock supplier for LNG Canada. Advisory implication: Barring a material adverse change or proxy war (neither currently signalled), the July 14 vote is the next binary milestone. A successful vote clears the path to close in Q3/Q4 2026, but regulatory approvals (particularly Investment Canada Act review in the context of energy security) remain a latent risk. Accenture's integration planning conversations with Shell's M&A and digital teams should accelerate post-vote, not post-close — the integration design window is July–September.

Shell announced on June 12 that it is pausing its current $3.0 billion share buyback programme — announced in May 2026 and tracking to completion by July 24 — with immediate effect through market close on July 14, 2026. The suspension is required under securities law in connection with ARC Resources filing its shareholder meeting circular (see item above). Any shares not repurchased during the blackout will be rolled into Shell's remaining 2026 buyback programmes, subject to board approval. Prior to the pause, Shell had been buying back approximately 950,000–1.25 million shares per day across LSE, Chi-X, and BATS venues at volume-weighted prices near £32.43–£32.47. The 32-calendar-day suspension represents a pause of approximately £950M in planned purchases at current share prices. Advisory implication: The buyback pause is technical and expected — it does not signal a change in capital return policy. The more important dynamic: if the ARC vote succeeds on July 14 and the Iran deal resolves Hormuz, Shell enters Q2 earnings (July 30) with two positive catalysts (buyback restart + production recovery) that could accelerate the share price re-rating toward analyst targets of £38–45.
