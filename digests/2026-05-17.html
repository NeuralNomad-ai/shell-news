1. Strategy & Corporate Moves

INEOS / OilPrice / Energy Live News · 6 May 2026 C-suiteNEW

Shell & INEOS Strike Gulf of America Deepwater Exploration Alliance

Shell and INEOS Energy have agreed to jointly pursue E&P opportunities in the deepwater U.S. Gulf of America, with INEOS acquiring a 21% working interest consistent with its existing stake in Appomattox, Rydberg, the Nashville discovery, and the Mattox pipeline. The initial programme covers three prospects: Shell's pre-FID Fort Sumter discovery, the Sisco exploration well, and a third well to be drilled by end-2030 — all targeted for tieback to the Shell-operated Appomattox hub. The tieback strategy keeps unit development costs low and leverages sunk infrastructure. For C-suite context: this deal, read alongside the ARC Resources acquisition, signals a deliberate barbell upstream strategy — low-cost Canadian gas/LNG optionality on one side, high-margin deepwater GoA on the other. INEOS's financial participation de-risks Shell's exploration spend.

2. Energy Transition & Sustainability

Shell.com / GreentechLead / WebWire · 14 May 2026 C-suiteNEW

Shell Publishes Climate & Energy Transition Lobbying Report 2025

Shell's eighth annual lobbying transparency report (published 14 May alongside the Government Payments Report) reviews 49 industry associations: 37 fully aligned, 12 partially aligned with Shell's climate positions — no associations rated misaligned this year, a marginal improvement. Key 2025 lobbying activities: successfully helped preserve the 45Q carbon sequestration tax credit and partially retain the 45V clean hydrogen credit under IRA discussions; supported 45Z SAF production incentives; and backed carbon pricing mechanisms (ETSs and carbon taxes) as a "central pillar" of net-zero policy. Shell also expanded global disclosure of all industry association memberships with ≥$50K annual fees. Accenture lens: this report is a key AGM-eve move — it pre-empts proxy advisor criticism on lobbying misalignment and reinforces the board's narrative that Shell actively engages policy levers rather than merely managing disclosure.

3. Financials & Earnings

Shell.com / Investing.com / StockTitan · 14 May 2026 C-suiteNEW

Shell Reports $23.84B in Government Payments for 2025 — Effective Tax Rate 39%

Shell's statutory Report on Payments to Governments for FY2025 (published 14 May) discloses $23.84B in total payments to governments across 26 countries. Breakdown: taxes $10.04B, production entitlements $8.04B, royalties $3.77B, fees $1.63B, bonuses $360M. Top five recipients: Qatar $2.91B, Malaysia $2.38B, Nigeria $2.02B, Australia $1.82B, USA $1.12B. Shell's effective tax rate of 39% — nearly double the OECD average of 20.5% — reflects Shell's exposure to high-royalty upstream jurisdictions. Accenture lens: with ARC Resources bringing Canada into scope at scale in H2 2026, the 2026 government payments report will show a significant shift in geographic distribution toward a new top-5 jurisdiction. Advisors should prepare C-suite for questions on Canada's fiscal regime and Montney royalty structures.
