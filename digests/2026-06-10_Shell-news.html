Hormuz Ceasefire Remains Fragile — Baker Hughes Says Full Reopening Unlikely Before H2 2026; Shell Qatar LNG Recovery Delayed

Despite the US-Iran ceasefire declared on 8 April, Strait of Hormuz shipping traffic remains far below pre-conflict levels — now entering its third month of severe disruption. Baker Hughes CEO Lorenzo Simonelli warned at end-April that "persistent risk premiums" will characterise both oil and LNG markets, and a Dallas Federal Reserve survey found ~80% of oil and gas executives expect the strait will not fully reopen until August or later. Shell's direct exposure: Pearl GTL Train 2 in Qatar requires a ~12-month repair (repair costs "well below" $500M); Shell declared force majeure on Qatari LNG contracts in March and Qatar's force majeure on 20% of global LNG supply persists. CFO Sinead Gorman flagged Qatar as the "most significant" Q2 headwind at the May 7 earnings call. The EIA's June 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook models supply shut-ins at 11.3M b/d with expectations of further rise through Q2. Advisory note for Accenture-Shell engagements: Shell's Q2 2026 earnings (July 30) will be the first full-quarter read on Hormuz impact; expect sell-side pressure on LNG volumes guidance and leverage ratios given net debt already up 27% YoY to $52.6B.

Shell's Sprng Energy India Sale Hits Roadblocks Despite Aditya Birla/KKR Exclusivity — Renewable Regulation Complicates ~$1.7B Exit

Shell's planned exit from Sprng Energy — India's 5 GWp renewables platform originally acquired from Actis for $1.55B in 2020 — is encountering execution complications despite entering exclusive talks with an Aditya Birla Group/KKR consortium at an enterprise value of approximately $1.7–2.0B. Indian renewable energy regulations impose right-of-first-refusal requirements and government notification obligations that are slowing deal closure. The strategic rationale for the divestment remains intact: Sawan is reallocating capital to LNG and upstream gas, particularly in the context of the Hormuz crisis which has re-priced gas globally. However, a delayed or failed sale leaves Shell holding a ~$400M book-value headache and complicates the clean capital narrative underpinning the ARC Resources financing. Advisory note: If the Birla/KKR deal slips to H1 2027, this asset will reappear as a distraction at Shell's Q3 strategy investor day. Watch for a potential price reduction to accelerate closure.

LNG Canada / Al Jazeera / GasWorld · 2026-06-10 update
Energy-Transition C-suite High Impact Direct

LNG Canada Train 2 Now Fully Operational as Asian Spot Prices Surge 140% Post-Ras Laffan — Shell's Gas-Over-Renewables Bet Vindicated

Both LNG Canada trains (combined 13 mtpa) are now in production — a milestone that sits atop a dramatically tightened LNG market. QatarEnergy's force majeure declared on 4 March removed approximately 20% of global LNG supply overnight following Iranian missile attacks on Ras Laffan. As a direct consequence, Asian LNG spot prices surged over 140%, and European LNG prices followed given the EU's 12–14% Qatar-source dependency. LNG Canada's Pacific Basin positioning makes it an immediate substitute supplier for North Asian buyers. Shell's Q1 2026 filings already noted LNG Canada ramp-up partially offsetting Qatar outages in liquefaction volumes. Combined with the pending $22B ARC Resources acquisition to deepen Montney gas reserves, Shell's deliberate pivot away from wind and solar toward integrated gas — heavily criticised by ESG investors in 2024-25 — has been operationally vindicated by the Hormuz crisis. OECD gas inventories are forecast by the EIA to reach 23-year lows by December 2026. Advisory note: This is a compelling C-suite narrative for Sawan at capital markets day; Accenture should prepare supporting analysis on LNG Canada's contribution to EU energy security for stakeholder presentations.

U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) · June 2026 STEO
Financials C-suite High Impact Context

EIA June 2026 STEO: Brent Falls to $107/b as Demand Revised Down 1.1M b/d; OECD Inventories Heading for 23-Year Low by December

The EIA's June 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook (freshly published) marks a significant downward revision to its demand outlook: global oil demand is now forecast to decrease by 1.1M b/d in 2026 versus the prior estimate of +0.2M b/d growth — a 1.3M b/d swing driven by demand destruction from elevated prices and trade friction. Brent crude averaged $107/b in May, down $10/b from April's peak as US-Iran ceasefire talks progressed, but the price remains ~25% above pre-conflict levels. Supply shut-ins averaging 11.3M b/d are expected to continue rising through Q2. Paradoxically, despite demand contraction, OECD inventories are forecast to fall to just under 2.3B barrels by December 2026 — the lowest since 2003 — because supply loss outpaces demand loss. Shell implications: Elevated Brent supports Shell's upstream earnings and justifies the ARC Resources premium; however demand destruction creates medium-term LNG demand uncertainty that complicates Sawan's 60% LNG growth-by-2040 thesis and could be a flashpoint at investor days. Wholesale gasoline now forecast at $2.98/gal in 2026, up ~$1.00/gal from the February STEO.
