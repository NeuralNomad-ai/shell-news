Shell Sues Woodside & Paladin in West Australian Supreme Court for $83M Northern Endeavour Decommissioning Levies

Shell has filed suit in the West Australian Supreme Court against Woodside Energy and Paladin Resources to recover two Australian Taxation Office levies — $41.2M (2024 assessment) and $42.4M (2025 assessment) — relating to the government-led decommissioning of the Northern Endeavour FPSO in the Timor Sea. Shell's legal argument rests on 2005 sale agreements under which the buyers allegedly assumed all future environmental and abandonment liabilities. The Northern Endeavour became a government liability after its private owner went insolvent in 2019; Australia has since spent over $700M on remediation. This litigation is notable for three reasons: (1) Shell is testing whether historical asset-sale indemnities are enforceable against insolvent chains — a precedent with global implications for divestiture structuring; (2) the outcome will define Shell's total decommissioning exposure in Australia; (3) it demonstrates that Shell is willing to pursue aggressive cost recovery to defend balance sheet discipline even post-portfolio exit. Advisory angle: Accenture legal and risk advisors can reference this case when supporting Shell on divestiture strategy — indemnity architecture in future asset sales will require careful drafting given the ARC integration generating new Australian-parallels in Canada.

Shell's REFHYNE 2: 100MW Rheinland Hydrogen Electrolyser Secures Wind and Solar PPAs — 16,000 Tonnes/Year H2 on Track for 2027

Shell Energy Europe has signed two power purchase agreements to supply renewable electricity to its 100MW REFHYNE 2 hydrogen electrolyser under construction at the Shell Energy and Chemicals Park Rheinland (Cologne), distinct from the Rotterdam Holland Hydrogen 1 project covered in prior digests. PPA 1: a five-year offshore wind deal with Nordsee One (332MW North Sea farm, JV of Northland Power & RWE), covering ~33% of that farm's output. PPA 2: a ten-year solar deal with Solarkraftwerk Halenbeck in Brandenburg (~75% offtake of a 230MW PV plant). At full capacity from 2027, REFHYNE 2 will produce up to 16,000 tonnes of green hydrogen per year — roughly 12x the output of its predecessor REFHYNE 1 — displacing grey hydrogen in the Rheinland refinery's operations. EU Horizon 2020 funding and German regulatory frameworks underpin the project. Context: Two distinct large-scale green hydrogen proof points operational within 12 months (Rotterdam H1 + Rheinland REFHYNE 2) significantly strengthens Shell's argument that its energy transition strategy is execution-stage rather than announcement-stage — which directly supports Shell's investor relations narrative post-AGM.

Shell's AI LNG Optimiser Commercialised: Real-Time Sensor-to-Algorithm Loop Cuts LNG Liquefaction Costs — Model for Agentic AI at Scale

Shell has commercialised its "Shell Process Optimiser for LNG" — an AI system that ingests real-time sensor data from LNG liquefaction plants and continuously calculates the most energy-efficient equipment configuration, reducing liquefaction energy costs (typically 6–10% of operating cost). The tool has been deployed across multiple Shell LNG assets and is now being offered to third-party LNG operators, creating a software revenue stream from Shell's operational IP. This mirrors the trajectory of other industrial AI leaders who built tools for internal use and then commercialised them. Technically, it represents a canonical agentic loop: sense (sensor data) → reason (AI model) → act (equipment setting adjustments) → observe (output measurement) → repeat. The Shell-SLB collaboration (Lumi platform, agentic AI for subsurface) follows the same pattern at a larger scale. Advisory angle: Accenture's AI practice can use Shell's LNG Optimiser as a concrete reference case when scoping agentic AI deployments with Shell's downstream and integrated gas teams — the architecture is proven, the ROI is measurable, and Shell has already accepted the "AI decides equipment settings" risk threshold. The expansion pathway is clear: replicate across all LNG assets globally, then integrate with the SLB Lumi platform for cross-asset learning.
